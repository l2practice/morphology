sumptuous	-sumptu- + -ous	presumptuous, sumptuously, sumptuary	From Middle French sumptueux, from Latin sumptuōsus, from sūmptus (“cost, charge, expense”), from sumō (“to take”) + -tus (noun formation suffix).
noxious	-noxi- + -ous	obnoxious, obnoxiously	From Latin noxius (“hurtful, injurious”), from noxa (“hurt, injury”), from nocere (“to hurt, injure”); see nocent.
judicious	-judici- + -ous	judicial, judiciary, prejudicial, judiciously	From French judicieux, ultimately derived from Latin iudico. Related to judge, judicial.

obedient	-obedi- + -ent	obedience, disobedience, obediently, disobedient	From Middle English obedient, from Old French obedient, from Latin oboediēns, present active participle of oboediō (“obey”).
